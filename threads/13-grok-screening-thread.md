# Thread: Using Grok as a Stock Screener
## Source: /Users/testuser/marketing/substack/drafts/13-grok-screening/draft.md

### Post 1 (Hook)
I asked Grok "what stocks are retail traders hyped about that Wall Street hasn't caught onto yet?"

One answer: @LUNR at $7. NASA contract catalyst. Revenue growing 270% YoY.

Two weeks later, contract announced. $7 → $12. 70% in fourteen days.

That signal was on Twitter days before any analyst note.

### Post 2
Grok as a language model is fine. Not the best.

But it's the only AI that reads Twitter in real time.

Ask ChatGPT what's trending — it says "I don't have real-time data." Ask Grok and it synthesizes what thousands of traders are actually saying right now, with tickers and catalysts.

### Post 3
The prompt that works best:

"What stocks are retail traders on X most excited about in the last 48 hours? Focus on names with actual catalysts, not just meme hype. Give me tickers, sentiment, and what people think the catalyst is."

"Actual catalysts" and "not just meme hype" are doing the heavy lifting.

### Post 4
The receipts:

@SMCI — Fintwit called it an AI infra play months before it 5x'd. @APP — Twitter darling weeks before it went $80 → $400+. @RKLB — retail pounded the table under $5, hit $25+.

But for every @RKLB, ten stocks Twitter loved went straight to hell. @NKLA, @RIDE, @MULN.

Social signal without fundamental validation is exit liquidity with rocket emojis.

### Post 5
Where Grok will absolutely burn you:

It hallucinates financials. Asked for revenue on a mid-cap last week — confidently wrong by 30%.

It reflects the crowd. If 10,000 people are irrationally bullish, Grok faithfully reports the excitement. It synthesizes sentiment. It does not correct it.

Never trust any number it gives you.

### Post 6 (Closer)
The workflow: Grok surfaces ideas. You filter hard. Validate fundamentals with real data. Size small — 2-3% max per social-sourced idea.

It turned my Twitter from a doomscroll into an edge. Just don't confuse the sentiment layer for analysis.
