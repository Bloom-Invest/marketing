# Thread: AI Replaces the $150K Junior Analyst
## Source: /Users/testuser/marketing/substack/drafts/05-desk-research/draft.md

### Post 1 (Hook)
Wall Street pays junior analysts $150K+ to copy numbers from Bloomberg into Excel, format slides, and highlight 3 paragraphs in a 180-page filing. Most of what they do isn't sophisticated intellectual work. It's mechanical. And AI agents now do that 80% in minutes.

### Post 2
Every retail investor already does Wall Street's "desk research" workflow — just a crappy version. Checking Yahoo Finance? That's data gathering. Reading a Reddit post about a stock? That's competitive analysis. Glancing at the P/E and deciding "seems reasonable"? That's financial modeling with 5% of the rigor.

### Post 3
The real win isn't speed — it's consistency. When I research manually, quality depends on my mood, my excitement about the stock, whether I remembered to check the balance sheet. AI runs the same process every time. No lazy Sunday shortcuts. No confirmation bias. No sunk-cost anchoring.

### Post 4
But the "AI replaces everything" narrative is bullshit. It can't read management quality. Can't feel competitive dynamics shifting before the numbers show it. Can't tell when a company is cooking the books. The best investors get intel from relationships — "I talked to three CIOs and none are renewing Salesforce." No API for that.

### Post 5
For decades, research quality tracked directly with AUM. Bloomberg terminals at $24K/year per seat. Analyst teams. Expert networks. The gap was never about intelligence — it was about resources. AI agents are collapsing that gap for the first time.

### Post 6 (Closer)
2019: three monitors, 1am, one stock memo after three days. Today the same workflow runs on your phone in minutes. AI handles the research. The decisions are still yours. That's not a limitation — that's the whole point.
