## What the Hell Does Palantir Actually Do?

Go read any Reddit thread about PLTR. Half the comments are about the stock price. Almost nobody can explain the business in plain English.

Palantir builds software that helps organizations make sense of massive, messy datasets. They have four products:

**Gotham** — built for government and intelligence agencies. CIA, NSA, military. Takes data from dozens of sources — satellite imagery, signals intelligence, informant reports, drone feeds — and lets analysts connect dots across all of it. The OG product, built starting in 2003 with CIA backing.

**Foundry** — the commercial version. Companies like Airbus or BP use it to unify operational data into one platform so people across the org can actually find and use it.

**Apollo** — their deployment layer. Pushes software updates across all environments, including classified government networks where you can't just `git push` to production.

**AIP** — the Artificial Intelligence Platform. Launched in 2023, it lets enterprises plug large language models directly into operational workflows. Not chatbot stuff — actual decision-making pipelines where AI agents do real work on real data. AIP is why the stock went parabolic.

The critical thing: Palantir doesn't sell data. They sell the *operating system* for your data. And their customers are extremely sticky — once you've rebuilt your entire decision-making infrastructure on Foundry or Gotham, ripping it out is like uninstalling your company's nervous system.

## Follow the Money

Okay, we know what they build. Who's paying and how much?

Palantir's TTM revenue as of Q4 2025: **$4.48 billion.** Up from $2.87B in 2024, up from $2.23B in 2023.

The quarterly trajectory is where it gets interesting:

- Q1 2025: $884M
- Q2 2025: $1.0B
- Q3 2025: $1.18B
- Q4 2025: $1.41B

That's not just growth. That's *accelerating* growth. Each quarter steps up $150-230M sequentially. For a company growing 17% in 2023, this acceleration is the entire story.

Revenue splits into two buckets: **government** and **commercial**. Historically, government was the majority — 60%+. But commercial has been growing faster, fueled by AIP. Companies sign up for AIP bootcamps (yes, that's what Palantir actually calls them), try the product for a few weeks, and convert to six- and seven-figure annual contracts.

**Profit margin: 36.3%.** Net income TTM of $1.63B. This company was *losing over a billion dollars a year* in 2020. In Q3 2025 alone: $476M in net income. They're sitting on **$7.18 billion** in cash with virtually no debt (3% debt-to-equity).

A company that burned cash for a decade, now swimming in it. That part of the story is unambiguously good.

## Skip the 10-K, Read the Earnings Call

Here's a tip that will save you dozens of hours: **don't start with the annual report.**

Every "how to research stocks" guide tells you to read the 10-K. Sure, the 10-K is thorough. It's also 200+ pages of SEC-mandated legalese written by lawyers for lawyers. If you're a retail investor trying to form an opinion on a Thursday night, it's a waste of your time.

The earnings call transcript is the cheat code. Every quarter, the CEO and CFO talk about what happened, what's coming, and what they're worried about. Analysts ask pointed, sometimes hostile questions. The whole thing reads in fifteen minutes.

What you're looking for:

1. **What is management excited about?** Not the scripted opening — the answers to questions. When the CEO goes off-script, that's where the signal is.
2. **What questions do analysts keep hammering?** If three analysts in a row hit the same topic, that's what the market cares about. For Palantir: AIP conversion rates and government pipeline.
3. **What did they *not* say?** If the CFO dances around a direct question about commercial margins, that's a flag. Silence is data.

Palantir's Q4 2025 call was basically an AIP victory lap. CEO Alex Karp talked about demand being "without precedent." But here's what I flagged: lots of time on US commercial growth, very little on international commercial. The US is carrying the load. International hasn't kicked in — either a risk or an opportunity, depending on your thesis.

## The Valuation Gut-Check

This is where most people either buy or walk away — usually for the wrong reasons.

Palantir's trailing P/E: **209x.** Forward P/E: **110x.** Price-to-sales: **75x.**

Your gut reaction: absolutely nuts. And it kind of is. Nosebleed multiples by any historical standard.

But here's what P/E screenshots on Twitter miss: you compare valuation to *growth*, not to some abstract sense of "expensive."

Revenue growth just printed 63% YoY. Operating income went from $113M to $393M — up 248%. Earnings are growing *faster* than revenue because of operating leverage: same infrastructure, more revenue, without proportionally more cost.

PEG ratio: ~2.8. For a high-growth software company with 36% net margins — not cheap, but not "this is a bubble" territory either. Datadog trades at 313x PE. Snowflake isn't even profitable.

Here's how I think about it: if Palantir does ~$6.5-7B in revenue in 2026 with 20%+ operating margins (already at 33% quarterly), you're looking at maybe $1.50-1.70 in EPS. At 60x earnings — still a premium — that's ~$100. At 80x, ~$130. At 100x, $160.

Current price of $131 basically prices in continued excellence AND a premium multiple. You're paying for perfection. If growth decelerates or margins compress, real downside.

**The valuation gut-check: the numbers justify the price only if the growth story keeps playing out.** That's not a slam dunk. It's a bet.

## The 2-3 Things That Actually Matter

Every stock comes down to a handful of key drivers. For Palantir, three:

**1. AIP adoption.** The big one. AIP turned Palantir from a slow-growing government contractor into a high-growth AI platform company. If bootcamp conversions stay strong and deal sizes expand, the growth story continues. If enterprise AI spending slows or competitors (Databricks, Snowflake, AWS) eat their lunch, it unravels.

**2. Government pipeline.** The foundation. Deep relationships across DoD, intelligence, and increasingly civilian agencies. Risk: budgets get cut, contracts delayed, priorities shift. Opportunity: AI in defense spending is *accelerating*, and Palantir is the incumbent.

**3. International commercial.** The sleeper. US commercial is firing on all cylinders. International is a much bigger addressable market, barely penetrated. If international starts growing at US rates, the revenue trajectory goes from impressive to absurd. But data sovereignty concerns, local competitors, and longer sales cycles could keep it slow.

Three things. Every quarter, check: Is AIP converting? Are government deals flowing? Is international waking up? Yes to all three, stock goes up. Any of them break, you've got a problem.

## Take a Damn Stance

Most research guides give you data and wimp out. "Do your own due diligence." Thanks, very helpful.

The whole point is to form a *thesis*. An opinion you're willing to put money behind.

**Bull case:** Palantir is one of maybe three companies (alongside Microsoft and Google) actually shipping real enterprise AI products — not demos, not prototypes, but software Fortune 500 companies deploy in production. AIP is a genuine platform shift. If it keeps converting and international opens up, this could be $500B+ in 2-3 years.

**Bear case:** You're paying 200x trailing earnings for a company that only recently became consistently profitable. CEO Alex Karp sells shares regularly. The stock has 10x'd from its 2023 lows. At $313B market cap on $4.5B revenue, any growth disappointment gets punished *brutally*. If AI spending cools, this stock easily loses 40-50%.

**My take:** Palantir is a legitimately great company with a legitimately terrifying valuation. The product is real. The growth is real. The margins are real. But you're paying for three years of flawless execution baked into today's price. I'd want to own it at $90-100, not $131. It goes on the watchlist. I wait for a pullback or an earnings miss that gives me a better entry.

That's a thesis. Specific, backed by numbers, actionable. That's what the process gives you.

## How Much to Buy

Position sizing — the most important and most ignored part of investing.

**High conviction + reasonable valuation = 5-8% of portfolio.** You've done the work, you believe the thesis, price isn't making you wince.

**High conviction + expensive = 2-4%.** Great company, nervous about the price. Palantir at $131 falls here.

**Speculative = 1-2%.** You're intrigued but not convinced. The "let me own a little so I pay attention" position.

For Palantir today: 2-3% bucket. Enough to matter if I'm right. Not enough to ruin my month if it drops 40% on an earnings miss. Plan to add if the stock comes in and the thesis holds.
