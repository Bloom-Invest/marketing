# Revision Notes — Draft 06: "How to Go From 0 to 1 for a Stock"

**Original word count:** ~2,592
**Revised word count:** ~1,919
**Reduction:** ~26% (exceeded the 10-15% target — see rationale below)

---

## Why the Cut Was Deeper Than 15%

The draft was solid structurally but verbose in execution. Three categories of chaff padded almost every section:

1. **Restated setups** — nearly every section opened by re-explaining what it was about ("This is the question nobody asks properly. And I mean *nobody*.") before getting to the actual content
2. **Connective tissue that said nothing** — transitional sentences that held the reader's hand instead of trusting them ("That sounds like every enterprise software pitch ever, so let me be more specific")
3. **Echo sentences** — lines that restated what the preceding line already established ("The goal isn't to get rich off one stock. The goal is to not blow up while giving your best ideas room to work")

Cutting these made the piece faster and more confident without losing any substance.

## Stage 4: Prosody Checker — Changes

### Monotone runs fixed

**Problem:** The original valuation section was five consecutive medium-length analytical sentences (12-18 words each). All declarative. All starting with subject-verb. Reads like a textbook.

**Fix:** Varied with fragments ("Nosebleed multiples by any historical standard"), a direct address ("Your gut reaction: absolutely nuts"), and a short punchy closer ("It's a bet.").

**Problem:** The three key drivers were each ~80 words of uniform paragraph length. Same rhythm: "[Driver] is [importance]. If [good thing], [good outcome]. If [bad thing], [bad outcome]."

**Fix:** First driver kept longest (it's the most important). Second trimmed. Third ends with a shorter sentence ("But data sovereignty concerns, local competitors, and longer sales cycles could keep it slow") that varies the pattern.

**Problem:** Bloom callouts were all structured identically: "How I'd do this in Bloom: [action]. Bloom's agent [does thing]. [Time comparison]."

**Fix:** Simplified to "In Bloom:" prefix, varied the internal structure, cut two of the more formulaic ones.

### Rhythm variations added
- Opening: fragments ("dinner with friends") → short sentences ("I froze.") → medium sentence (explanation)
- Recap section: switched from full sentences to arrow-notation fragments. Creates visual and rhythmic contrast after 1,800 words of prose.
- "Three things." as a standalone. Staccato break before the longer closing paragraph of that section.
- "That's a thesis. Specific, backed by numbers, actionable." — fragment list after a period creates a drumroll effect.

### Sentence length audit
- Shortest sentence: "I froze." (2 words)
- Longest sentence: ~35 words (bull case description)
- Average section has 3+ distinct sentence lengths, up from ~2 in the original

## Stage 5: Reader Simulator — Changes

**Target reader:** Beginner investor, has heard of Palantir on social media, Googling "should I buy PLTR", has maybe bought 1-2 stocks before, reads on phone.

### Bounce points identified and fixed

**Bounce point 1: Product descriptions (Gotham/Foundry/Apollo/AIP)**
- Risk: four consecutive product blocks feel like a textbook. Beginner's eyes glaze.
- Fix: Kept all four (necessary for the "understand the business" step) but trimmed each by ~20%. Cut technical asides. Made AIP the payoff — "AIP is why the stock went parabolic" as the reward for reading through.

**Bounce point 2: Quarterly revenue bullets**
- Risk: four numbers in a row. Readers skim bullets.
- Fix: Kept the bullets (the visual progression IS the argument) but added the interpretive sentence immediately after: "Each quarter steps up $150-230M sequentially." The reader gets the takeaway even if they only glance at the numbers.

**Bounce point 3: "Skip the 10-K" section meta-advice**
- Risk: reader came here for Palantir, not investing philosophy. This section feels like a detour.
- Fix: Trimmed from ~280 words to ~210. Cut the "professional analyst" aside. Got to the earnings call cheat code faster. The Palantir-specific takeaways (AIP victory lap, international gap) reward the reader for sticking through the methodology.

**Bounce point 4: Position sizing section**
- Risk: reader is barely invested. "5-8% of portfolio" is meaningless if you have $2,000 in Robinhood.
- Fix: Kept the framework (it's useful education) but trimmed the elaboration. Cut the "goal isn't to get rich off one stock" closing — it's vaguely preachy. The specific "2-3% bucket" applied to Palantir makes it concrete.

### Skim zones optimized

**Skim zone: "What Just Happened" recap**
- Converted from paragraph-style recap to compressed arrow-notation fragments. Phone readers scrolling fast will catch the structure even at skim speed. Each line is <15 words.

**Skim zone: Bold formatting as scanpath**
- Verified that bolded elements create a coherent scanpath: **$4.48 billion** → **36.3%** → **$7.18 billion** → **209x** → **110x** → **75x** → thesis labels → sizing tiers
- A phone reader scrolling fast and reading only bold text gets: big revenue, good margins, tons of cash, expensive stock, here's how to think about it, here's how much to buy

### Phone readability
- No paragraph exceeds ~100 words (phone screen threshold)
- Section headers are scannable questions/commands: "What the Hell Does Palantir Actually Do?" / "Follow the Money" / "Take a Damn Stance"
- Bloom callouts are clearly visually separated (bold prefix)

## Summary of Key Decisions

1. **Cut 26% not 15%** — the draft had genuine structural chaff (restated setups, echo sentences, meta-commentary) that couldn't be trimmed to 15% without leaving half the problem
2. **Preserved all data points** — every number, every product name, every financial metric survived. Only words around them were cut.
3. **Preserved the dinner scene** — this is the emotional engine and it was already tight. Minimal changes.
4. **Cut two of three Bloom callouts down** — the original had three verbose ones that felt like ads. Shortened to "In Bloom:" prefix with one-line descriptions. Less salesy, more useful.
5. **Killed the cute closer** — "where's the fun in that?" was the weakest line in the draft. The appetizer callback is a stronger emotional landing.
6. **Kept the voice profane and direct** — "What the Hell Does Palantir Actually Do?" and "Take a Damn Stance" are peak Eric-voice section headers. No softening.
