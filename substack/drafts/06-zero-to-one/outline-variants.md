# Article #6 — Outline Variants

## Outline A: "The Reddit DM Workflow"
**Hook:** Friend DMs you a ticker on Reddit. You know nothing. Walk through the exact steps from ignorance to informed opinion.
**Structure:**
1. Open with the "my buddy texted me PLTR" anecdote
2. Step 1: What does this company actually do? (harder than it sounds)
3. Step 2: Find the business model — how do they make money?
4. Step 3: Skip the 10-K, read the earnings call
5. Step 4: Valuation vs. growth — the only ratio that matters
6. Step 5: Identify 2-3 key drivers
7. Step 6: Form a thesis (bull case, bear case)
8. Step 7: Position sizing — how much to actually buy
9. Close with "you now know more than 90% of retail investors"

**Pros:** Very linear, easy to follow, mirrors real user journey
**Cons:** Could feel listicle-adjacent if not written carefully

---

## Outline B: "The Palantir Deep Dive as Teaching Vehicle" ✅ CHOSEN
**Hook:** Someone at a dinner party asks about Palantir. You freeze. Walk through how to go from "I've heard of it" to an actual opinion using PLTR as the real-time example woven throughout.
**Structure:**
1. Open with the dinner party / group chat moment — everyone has opinions, you have nothing
2. "What the hell does Palantir actually do?" — the first question nobody asks properly
3. Follow the money — business model, revenue mix, who's paying
4. The earnings call trick — why the transcript beats the 10-K
5. The valuation gut-check — 200x PE sounds insane until you look closer (or does it?)
6. Key drivers — the 2-3 things that will make or break the thesis
7. Forming your thesis — taking a damn stance
8. Position sizing — the part everyone skips
9. Close: how Bloom collapses this from days to minutes

**Pros:** Single stock narrative is more engaging, controversial pick generates interest, teaches by showing not telling
**Cons:** Readers might think article is "about PLTR" rather than the process

---

## Outline C: "Two Stocks, Two Outcomes"
**Hook:** Open with researching two stocks — one that looked great on the surface (turned out overvalued), one that looked sketchy (turned out to be a gem). Show the process catches what vibes miss.
**Structure:**
1. Open with "I almost bought X because Reddit loved it. Here's what the numbers actually said."
2. The surface-level trap — why vibes-based investing fails
3. The 7-step research process (compressed, less detail per step)
4. Apply it to Stock A (the trap) — walk through the red flags
5. Apply it to Stock B (the gem) — walk through the green flags
6. Compare: same process, different outcomes, that's the point
7. How Bloom automates the boring parts
8. Close: the process is the edge, not the pick

**Pros:** Dramatic contrast is compelling, shows process works both ways
**Cons:** Too many stocks dilutes depth, harder to go deep on any single name, longer to write well

---

## Decision: Outline B

Outline B wins because:
- A single stock example lets us go *deep* rather than wide, which matches the "0 to 1" premise
- Palantir is perfect: it's divisive, widely discussed, genuinely complex, and the valuation question is fascinating
- The dinner party hook is universally relatable — everyone's been caught without an opinion
- It avoids the listicle feel of Outline A while being more focused than Outline C
- The data is rich enough to make every section concrete with real numbers
