# I Asked Grok "What Stocks Are Retail Traders Hyped About?" and Actually Made Money on One

*Twitter knows things before Bloomberg does. The trick is filtering signal from 10,000 rocket emojis.*

---

Three weeks ago I opened Grok on X and typed, mostly as a joke: "What stocks are retail traders on Twitter excited about right now that Wall Street hasn't caught onto yet?"

I expected meme stock pumps and crypto shills.

Instead, Grok came back with five tickers — each with a summary of *why* Twitter was buzzing. Not just "people are talking about it." Actual catalysts. New product launches. Earnings whispers. Short squeeze setups.

One was @LUNR — Intuitive Machines, the lunar lander company. Grok flagged retail excitement about an upcoming NASA contract announcement. Stock was sitting around $7.

I pulled it up in [Bloom](https://investwithbloom.com). Revenue growing 270% YoY. Cash runway through 2026. Institutional ownership ticking up. The NASA catalyst was real — not a rumor, an actual scheduled announcement.

I bought a small position. Two weeks later, the contract hit. @LUNR ran to $12. 70% in fourteen days.

That signal was on Twitter *days* before any analyst note or Bloomberg headline. Grok surfaced it without me scrolling through 10,000 tweets from people with laser eyes in their profile pics.

That's when I started treating Grok as a stock screener — the slightly unhinged research assistant who hangs out in the bar where all the traders drink and reports back on what they're whispering about.

## Twitter Is a Leading Indicator. Seriously.

When @DJT started moving in late 2024 — Twitter first. When retail piled into @SMCI before the big run — Twitter. When Roaring Kitty came back and @GME ripped 120% in a day, did you first hear about it on CNBC?

No. CNBC covered it three hours later with a confused anchor asking what a "short squeeze" is.

X is the fastest public channel for retail stock sentiment. Faster than news wires. Faster than your broker's "market insights" email that arrives at 9:30am after everything already moved.

The problem: stock Twitter is a nightmare. For every person posting real analysis, there are 50 posting rocket emojis and "TRUST THE PROCESS 🚀🚀🚀" and screenshots of paper trading accounts. Signal-to-noise: maybe 5%.

## Why Grok, Not ChatGPT

Grok as a language model is fine. Not the best — Claude is smarter for reasoning, GPT-4o more polished for general tasks. I don't care about Grok's raw intelligence.

I care about one thing: **it reads Twitter in real time.**

Ask ChatGPT "what stocks are trending on social media?" and it tells you it doesn't have real-time data. Suggests Google Trends. Thanks.

Ask Grok the same question — it *searches live posts on X*, synthesizes what thousands of people are saying, identifies the tickers getting mentioned most, and explains the *why* behind the sentiment. Not yesterday's news. Bar chatter as it happens.

Think of it as a research assistant who follows 10,000 stock accounts on Twitter, reads every tweet, filters the obvious garbage, and gives you a daily briefing. That assistant doesn't cost $150,000 a year. It's free with X Premium.

Perplexity can search the web — but it pulls from articles and blogs. Curated, edited, delayed. Grok pulls from the raw firehose of real-time human sentiment. Messier, but faster.

## The Prompts That Actually Work

I've tested dozens. Most produce garbage. Four consistently return something useful:

**"What's buzzing?"**
> "What stocks are retail traders on X most excited about in the last 48 hours? Focus on names with actual catalysts being discussed, not just meme hype. Give me tickers, the sentiment, and what people think the catalyst is."

"Actual catalysts" and "not just meme hype" are doing the heavy lifting here. Without them, you get whatever garbage is trending.

**"Undiscovered"**
> "What small-cap or mid-cap stocks are getting increasing attention on X this week that most institutional investors probably aren't watching yet? I want names under $10B market cap with real business momentum."

This one's gold for finding ideas early. The market cap filter pushes Grok away from @AAPL and @TSLA — stocks where Twitter sentiment tells you nothing useful — toward smaller names where social buzz can actually lead price.

**"Contrarian"**
> "What stocks are people on X most bearish about right now where the fundamentals might not justify the negativity? I'm looking for names where the hate might be overdone."

When everyone on fintwit is dunking on a stock, sometimes it's genuinely broken. Sometimes the crowd is wrong.

**"Sector rotation"**
> "Based on conversations on X over the past week, what sectors or themes are traders rotating into? What are they rotating out of? Give me specific names."

Catches theme shifts early. When Twitter starts buzzing about uranium or defense names, Grok flags it before it shows up in ETF flow data.

**One critical add-on:** Always append "give me the bear case too." Grok's default mode reflects the enthusiasm it sees in tweets. Forcing it to surface risks makes the output 10x more useful.

## 90% Is Still Garbage

Good prompts help. Maybe 1 in 5 ideas Grok surfaces is worth pursuing. My filter:

**The emoji test.** If the conversation around a stock is mostly 🚀🚀🚀 with no substantive analysis, you're not early. You're exit liquidity.

**Check the timeline.** Stock already up 40% this month? You missed it. I ask Grok directly: "How long has this been trending on X? Is this new buzz or weeks old?"

**Verify the catalyst independently.** Twitter loves rumors. "I heard from a guy who knows a guy that @AAPL is buying this company." Before I act on any Grok idea, I confirm the catalyst exists outside of Twitter. Is there actually an FDA decision coming? Is the earnings date real?

**Run it through Bloom.** This is the step that matters most.

## Grok Finds It, Bloom Validates It

My actual workflow. Ten minutes per idea:

**Grok surfaces 3-5 names.** Run a prompt.

**Quick sanity check.** Ask Grok follow-ups: "How long has [ticker] been trending? What's the bear case? Who are the most credible people discussing this?" Two minutes. Kills the obvious pump-and-dumps.

**Pull it up in Bloom.** Type the ticker into [Bloom](https://investwithbloom.com) and get the full financial picture — revenue trends, margins, cash flow, valuation, insider activity. Stuff Grok *cannot reliably give you*.

**Do the fundamentals support the hype?** Twitter excited about a new product launch but the company is bleeding cash with declining revenue? That hype is hot air. Growing revenue, expanding margins, insiders buying? You might have something.

**Size accordingly.** A Twitter-sourced idea gets 2-3% of my portfolio. Max. This isn't how I build core positions. It's how I find ideas I'd never see otherwise and take calculated swings.

Grok is the idea generation layer. Bloom is the validation layer. Grok without fundamental validation is gambling. Fundamental analysis without social sentiment is fine — but you'll miss things hiding in plain sight.

## The Receipts

Theory is cheap. Here are names where Twitter buzz preceded real moves:

**@SMCI (Super Micro Computer)** — Fintwit was calling this an AI infrastructure play in early 2024, months before the stock 5x'd. Thesis: someone has to build the servers for all this AI stuff. Analysts caught up later.

**@APP (AppLovin)** — Twitter darling for weeks before it broke out in late 2024. The conversation: AI-driven ad platform, massive cash generation. Stock went from $80 to $400+. Social signal was early and right.

**@RKLB (Rocket Lab)** — Retail traders pounded the table throughout 2024 as a "cheaper SpaceX" play. Under $5 when the chatter started. Above $25 by early 2025.

The graveyard is bigger: **for every @RKLB, ten stocks Twitter loved went straight to hell.** @NKLA, @RIDE, @HYMC, @MULN. Social excitement without fundamental support is collective delusion with rocket emojis.

That's why you need both layers.

## Where Grok Will Screw You

I should be honest about this part, because I've been burned:

**It hallucinates financial data.** I asked for revenue and earnings on a mid-cap last week. The numbers were confidently wrong — off by 30%. Not a rounding error. Fabricated. **Never trust Grok for specific financials.** That's why I run everything through Bloom.

**It reflects the crowd's biases.** If 10,000 people are irrationally bullish together, Grok faithfully reports that they're very excited. It synthesizes sentiment. It doesn't correct it.

**It can't tell you when.** Twitter was bullish on some AI stocks for months before they moved. Being early and being wrong feel identical until the stock proves you right.

**Its "analysis" is shallow.** Push Grok to analyze a stock rather than report sentiment, and you get "strong growth potential" and "attractive valuation" — no real math behind it. It's a sentiment reader, not an analyst. Use it as one.

**The paywall.** X Premium runs $8-16/month. Whether that's worth it depends on how often you use this workflow. For me it's a rounding error. For the principle of paying Elon — I try not to think about that.

## The Bottom Line

Grok is the only AI tool that gives you real-time social sentiment on stocks in a usable format. Not smarter than Claude. Not more accurate than ChatGPT. But it tells you what 10,000 traders are excited about *right now*, and nothing else does that.

The workflow: Grok finds ideas. You filter hard. [Bloom](https://investwithbloom.com) validates fundamentals. You size small and accept that most social-sourced ideas won't pan out.

It turned Twitter from a doomscroll into an edge.

Just don't trust any number it gives you.
