# I Asked Grok "What Stocks Are Retail Traders Hyped About?" and Actually Made Money on One

*Twitter knows things before Bloomberg does. The trick is filtering signal from 10,000 rocket emojis.*

---

Three weeks ago I opened Grok on X and typed, mostly as a joke: "What stocks are retail traders on Twitter excited about right now that Wall Street hasn't caught onto yet?"

I expected meme stock pumps and crypto shills.

Grok came back with five tickers — each with a summary of *why* Twitter was buzzing. Not "people are talking about it." Actual catalysts. Product launches. Earnings whispers. Short squeeze setups.

One was @LUNR — Intuitive Machines, the lunar lander company. Grok flagged retail excitement about an upcoming NASA contract announcement. Stock sitting around $7.

I pulled it up in [Bloom](https://investwithbloom.com). Revenue growing 270% YoY. Cash runway through 2026. Institutional ownership ticking up. The NASA catalyst was real — not a rumor, an actual scheduled announcement.

Bought a small position. Two weeks later, the contract hit. @LUNR ran to $12.

70% in fourteen days.

That signal was on Twitter *days* before any analyst note or Bloomberg headline. Grok surfaced it without me scrolling through 10,000 tweets from accounts with laser eyes in their profile pics.

That's when I started treating Grok as a stock screener. The slightly unhinged research assistant who hangs out in the bar where all the traders drink and reports back on what they're whispering about.

## Twitter Moves First

When @DJT started moving in late 2024 — Twitter first. When retail piled into @SMCI before the big run — Twitter. When Roaring Kitty came back and @GME ripped 120% in a day, did you first hear about it on CNBC?

CNBC covered it three hours later with a confused anchor asking what a "short squeeze" is.

X is the fastest public channel for retail stock sentiment. Faster than news wires. Faster than your broker's "market insights" email that arrives at 9:30am after everything already moved.

The problem: stock Twitter is a nightmare. For every person posting real analysis, fifty are posting rocket emojis and "TRUST THE PROCESS 🚀🚀🚀" with screenshots of paper trading accounts. Signal-to-noise ratio: maybe 5%.

## Why Grok, Not ChatGPT

Grok as a language model is fine. Not the best — Claude is smarter for reasoning, GPT-4o more polished for general tasks. I don't care about Grok's raw intelligence.

I care about one thing: **it reads Twitter in real time.**

Ask ChatGPT "what stocks are trending on social media?" It tells you it doesn't have real-time data. Suggests Google Trends. Thanks.

Ask Grok the same question and it *searches live posts on X*, synthesizes what thousands of people are saying, identifies the tickers getting mentioned most, and explains the *why* behind the buzz. Not yesterday's news. Bar chatter as it happens.

It's like a research assistant who follows 10,000 stock accounts, reads every tweet, filters the obvious garbage, and gives you a daily briefing. That assistant doesn't cost $150,000 a year. Free with X Premium.

Perplexity can search the web, but it pulls from articles and blogs. Curated, edited, delayed. Grok pulls from the raw firehose of real-time human sentiment. Messier. Faster.

## The Prompts That Actually Work

I've tested dozens. Most produce garbage. Four consistently return something useful.

**"What's buzzing?"**
> "What stocks are retail traders on X most excited about in the last 48 hours? Focus on names with actual catalysts being discussed, not just meme hype. Give me tickers, the sentiment, and what people think the catalyst is."

"Actual catalysts" and "not just meme hype" are doing the heavy lifting. Without those two phrases, you get whatever trash is trending.

**"Undiscovered"**
> "What small-cap or mid-cap stocks are getting increasing attention on X this week that most institutional investors probably aren't watching yet? I want names under $10B market cap with real business momentum."

Gold for finding ideas early. The market cap filter pushes Grok away from @AAPL and @TSLA — stocks where Twitter sentiment tells you nothing useful — toward smaller names where social buzz can actually lead price.

**"Contrarian"**
> "What stocks are people on X most bearish about right now where the fundamentals might not justify the negativity? I'm looking for names where the hate might be overdone."

Sometimes everyone dunking on a stock is justified. Sometimes the crowd is wrong.

**"Sector rotation"**
> "Based on conversations on X over the past week, what sectors or themes are traders rotating into? What are they rotating out of? Give me specific names."

Catches theme shifts early. When Twitter starts buzzing about uranium or defense names, Grok flags it before the moves show up in ETF flow data.

**One thing I always append:** "Give me the bear case too." Grok's default mode reflects the enthusiasm it finds in tweets. Forcing it to surface risks makes the output dramatically more useful.

## Most of It Is Still Garbage

Good prompts help. Maybe 1 in 5 ideas Grok surfaces is worth a second look. My filter:

**The emoji test.** If the conversation around a stock is mostly 🚀🚀🚀 with zero substantive analysis, you're not early. You're exit liquidity.

**Check the timeline.** Stock already up 40% this month? You missed it. I ask Grok directly: "How long has this been trending on X? Is this new buzz or weeks old?"

**Verify the catalyst independently.** Twitter loves rumors. "I heard from a guy who knows a guy that @AAPL is buying this company." Before I act on anything Grok surfaces, I confirm the catalyst exists outside of Twitter. Is there actually an FDA decision coming? Is the earnings date real? Can I find a press release?

**Run it through Bloom.** This is the step that matters most.

## Grok Finds It, Bloom Validates It

My actual workflow. About ten minutes per idea.

**Grok surfaces 3-5 names.** Run a prompt from above.

**Quick sanity check.** Ask Grok follow-ups: "How long has [ticker] been trending? What's the bear case? Who are the most credible people discussing this?" Two minutes. Kills the obvious pump-and-dumps.

**Pull it up in Bloom.** Type the ticker into [Bloom](https://investwithbloom.com) and get the full financial picture — revenue trends, margins, cash flow, valuation, insider activity. Stuff Grok *cannot reliably give you*.

**Do the fundamentals match the hype?** Twitter excited about a new product launch but the company is bleeding cash with declining revenue? Hot air. Revenue growing, margins expanding, insiders buying? Now I'm interested.

**Size accordingly.** A Twitter-sourced idea gets 2-3% of my portfolio. Max. This isn't how I build core positions. It's how I find ideas I'd never encounter otherwise and take a small, calculated swing.

Grok is the idea layer. Bloom is the validation layer. Grok without fundamental validation is gambling. Fundamental analysis without social sentiment is fine — but you'll miss things hiding in plain sight.

## The Receipts

Talk is cheap. Here's what Twitter caught early:

**@SMCI (Super Micro Computer)** — Fintwit called this an AI infrastructure play in early 2024, months before the stock 5x'd. The thesis: someone has to build the servers for all this AI stuff. Analysts caught up later.

**@APP (AppLovin)** — Twitter darling for weeks before it broke out in late 2024. The conversation: AI-driven ad platform, massive cash generation. $80 to $400+. Social was early and right.

**@RKLB (Rocket Lab)** — Retail traders pounded the table throughout 2024 calling it a "cheaper SpaceX" play. Under $5 when the chatter started. Above $25 by early 2025.

Now the graveyard. It's bigger.

**For every @RKLB, ten stocks Twitter loved went straight to hell.** @NKLA, @RIDE, @HYMC, @MULN. Social excitement without fundamental support is collective delusion with rocket emojis.

That's why you need both layers.

## Where Grok Will Screw You

I've been burned. I should be honest about it.

**It hallucinates financials.** I asked for revenue and earnings on a mid-cap last week. Confidently wrong — off by 30%. Not a rounding error. Made up. **Never trust Grok for specific financial data.** That's why everything goes through Bloom.

**It reflects the crowd.** If 10,000 people are irrationally bullish together, Grok faithfully reports that they're very excited. It synthesizes sentiment. It does not correct it.

**It can't tell you when.** Twitter was bullish on certain AI stocks for months before they moved. Being early and being wrong feel identical right up until the stock proves you right.

**Its "analysis" is shallow.** Push Grok to actually analyze a stock — not just report sentiment — and you get "strong growth potential" and "attractive valuation." No math behind it. It's a sentiment reader, not an analyst. Use it as one.

**The paywall.** X Premium runs $8-16/month. Whether that's worth it depends on how often you run this workflow. For me it's a rounding error. For the principle of paying Elon — I try not to think about that part.

## What This Actually Is

Grok is the only AI tool that gives you real-time social sentiment on stocks in a usable format. Not smarter than Claude. Not more accurate than ChatGPT. But it tells you what 10,000 traders are excited about *right now*, and nothing else does that as cleanly.

The workflow: Grok finds ideas. You filter hard. [Bloom](https://investwithbloom.com) validates fundamentals. You size small and accept that most social-sourced ideas won't work out.

It turned my Twitter from a doomscroll into an edge.

Just never trust any number it gives you.
