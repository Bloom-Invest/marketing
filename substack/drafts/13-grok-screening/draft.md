# I Asked Grok "What Stocks Are Retail Traders Hyped About?" and Actually Made Money on One

*Twitter knows things before Bloomberg does. The trick is filtering signal from 10,000 rocket emojis.*

---

Three weeks ago I opened Grok on X and typed, mostly as a joke: "What stocks are retail traders on Twitter excited about right now that Wall Street hasn't caught onto yet?"

I expected meme stock pumps and crypto shills.

Instead, Grok came back with five tickers, each with a summary of *why* Twitter was buzzing — not just "people are talking about it" but actual catalysts. New product launches. Earnings whispers. Short squeeze setups.

One was LUNR — Intuitive Machines, the lunar lander company. Grok flagged retail excitement about an upcoming NASA contract announcement. The stock was sitting around $7.

I pulled it up in [Bloom](https://investwithbloom.com). Revenue growing 270% YoY. Cash runway through 2026. Institutional ownership ticking up. The NASA catalyst was real — not a rumor, an actual scheduled announcement.

I bought a small position. Two weeks later, the contract hit. LUNR ran to $12. A 70% move in fourteen days.

That signal was sitting on Twitter *days* before any analyst note or Bloomberg headline. Grok surfaced it without making me scroll through 10,000 tweets from people with laser eyes in their profile pics.

That's when I started thinking of Grok as a stock screening tool — the slightly unhinged research assistant who hangs out in the bar where all the traders drink and reports back on what they're whispering about.

## Twitter Knows Things. Bloomberg Knows Them Later.

When DJT (Trump Media) started moving in late 2024, where did the momentum start? Twitter. When retail piled into SMCI before the big run? Twitter. When Roaring Kitty came back and GME ripped 120% in a day, did you first hear about it on CNBC?

No. CNBC covered it three hours later with a confused anchor asking what a "short squeeze" is.

X/Twitter is the fastest public channel for retail stock sentiment. Faster than news wires. Faster than your broker's "market insights" email that arrives at 9:30am after everything already moved.

The problem: swimming through stock Twitter is a nightmare. For every person posting genuine analysis, there are 50 posting rocket emojis and "TRUST THE PROCESS 🚀🚀🚀" and screenshots of paper trading accounts. Signal-to-noise ratio: maybe 5%.

That's where Grok gets interesting.

## What Makes Grok Different (It's Not the Model)

Grok as a language model is fine. Not the best — Claude is smarter for reasoning, GPT-4o more polished for general tasks. Grok's raw intelligence isn't why I'm writing this.

What makes Grok useful for stock screening is one thing: **it reads Twitter in real time.**

When I ask ChatGPT "what stocks are trending on social media?" it tells me it doesn't have real-time data and suggests I check Google Trends. Thanks.

When I ask Grok the same question, it *searches live posts on X*, synthesizes what thousands of people are saying, identifies the tickers being mentioned most, and explains the *why* behind the sentiment. Not yesterday's news. The bar chatter as it happens.

Grok is a research assistant who follows 10,000 stock accounts on Twitter, reads every tweet, filters the obvious garbage, and gives you a daily briefing. That assistant doesn't cost $150,000 a year. It's free with X Premium.

Perplexity can search the web, sure — but it pulls from articles and blogs. Curated, edited, delayed. Grok pulls from the raw firehose of real-time human sentiment. Messier, but *faster*.

## The Prompts That Actually Work

I've tested dozens. Most produce garbage. Four consistently return something useful:

**"What's buzzing?"**
> "What stocks are retail traders on X most excited about in the last 48 hours? Focus on names with actual catalysts being discussed, not just meme hype. Give me tickers, the sentiment, and what people think the catalyst is."

The phrases "actual catalysts" and "not just meme hype" are doing the heavy lifting. Without them, you get a list of whatever garbage is trending.

**"Undiscovered"**
> "What small-cap or mid-cap stocks are getting increasing attention on X this week that most institutional investors probably aren't watching yet? I want names under $10B market cap with real business momentum."

This one's gold for finding ideas early. The market cap filter pushes Grok away from AAPL and TSLA — stocks where Twitter sentiment tells you nothing useful — and toward smaller names where social buzz can actually lead price.

**"Contrarian"**
> "What stocks are people on X most bearish about right now where the fundamentals might not justify the negativity? I'm looking for names where the hate might be overdone."

When everyone on fintwit is dunking on a stock, sometimes it's genuinely broken. Sometimes the crowd is wrong. Grok helps you find the latter.

**"Sector rotation"**
> "Based on conversations on X over the past week, what sectors or themes are traders rotating into? What are they rotating out of? Give me specific names."

Catches theme shifts early. When Twitter starts talking about uranium or defense names, Grok flags it before it shows up in ETF flow data.

**One critical add-on:** Always append "give me the bear case too" to any prompt. Grok's default mode reflects the enthusiasm it sees in tweets. Forcing it to surface risks makes the output 10x more useful.

## 90% Is Still Garbage (The Filter)

Even with good prompts, maybe 1 in 5 ideas Grok surfaces is worth pursuing. Here's how I filter:

**The emoji test.** If the Twitter conversation around a stock is mostly 🚀🚀🚀 with no substantive analysis, you're not early. You're exit liquidity.

**Check the timeline.** If the stock is already up 40% this month, you missed it. I ask Grok directly: "How long has this been trending on X? Is this new buzz or weeks old?"

**Verify the catalyst independently.** Twitter loves rumors. "I heard from a guy who knows a guy that AAPL is buying this company." Before I act on any Grok idea, I confirm the catalyst exists. Is there actually an FDA decision coming? Is the earnings date real?

**Run it through Bloom.** This is the step that separates "interesting Twitter idea" from "actual investment thesis."

## Grok Finds It, Bloom Validates It

My actual workflow. Ten minutes per idea.

**Grok surfaces the idea.** Run a prompt, get 3-5 names.

**Quick sanity check.** Ask Grok follow-ups: "How long has [ticker] been trending? What's the bear case? Who are the most credible people discussing this?" Two minutes. Eliminates the obvious pump-and-dumps.

**Pull it up in Bloom.** Type the ticker into [Bloom](https://investwithbloom.com) and get the full financial picture — revenue trends, margins, cash flow, valuation, insider activity. Stuff Grok *cannot reliably provide*.

**Does the fundamental story support the hype?** If Twitter is excited about a new product launch but the company is bleeding cash with declining revenue, the hype is hot air. If the fundamentals confirm the thesis — growing revenue, expanding margins, insiders buying — you might have something.

**Size accordingly.** A Twitter-sourced idea gets 2-3% of my portfolio. Max. This isn't how I build core positions. It's how I find ideas I'd have missed and take calculated swings.

**Grok is the idea generation layer. Bloom is the validation layer.** Grok without fundamental validation is gambling. Fundamental analysis without social sentiment is fine — but you'll miss things hiding in plain sight on Twitter.

## Where Grok Will Screw You

**It hallucinates financial data.** I asked for revenue and earnings on a mid-cap last week. The numbers were confidently wrong — off by 30%. Not a rounding error. Fabricated. **Never trust Grok for specific financial figures.** This is why I run everything through Bloom.

**It reflects the crowd's biases.** If 10,000 people are irrationally bullish together, Grok faithfully reports that they're very excited. It synthesizes sentiment. It doesn't correct it.

**It can't tell you when.** Twitter was bullish on some AI stocks for months before they moved. Being early and being wrong feel identical until the stock proves you right.

**Its "analysis" is shallow.** Push Grok to analyze a stock rather than report sentiment, and it gives you "strong growth potential" and "attractive valuation" — without doing real math. It's a sentiment reader, not an analyst.

**The paywall.** You need X Premium ($8-16/month). Whether that's worth it depends on how often you use this workflow. For me it's a rounding error. For the principle of paying Elon — I try not to think about that.

Grok is the only AI tool that gives you real-time social sentiment on stocks in a usable format. Not smarter than Claude. Not more accurate than ChatGPT. But it tells you what 10,000 traders are excited about *right now*, and nothing else can do that.

The workflow: Grok finds ideas. You filter aggressively. [Bloom](https://investwithbloom.com) validates fundamentals. You size small and accept that most social-sourced ideas won't pan out.

It turned Twitter from a doomscroll into an edge.
