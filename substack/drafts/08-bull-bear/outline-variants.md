# Article #8: Bull vs Bear Case — Outline Variants

## Outline A: "The Conviction Trap" (Personal Bias Story)

**Hook:** The time I was dead wrong on a stock I was sure about — lost money because I only ever looked at the bull case.
1. Why we only see one side (confirmation bias, motivated reasoning, the echo chamber of fintwit)
2. The structure of a good bull/bear framework — growth drivers, risks, base/bull/bear targets, "what would change my mind"
3. How to use AI to argue against your own thesis — prompting techniques
4. Real example: walking through PLTR bull and bear case side by side
5. How Bloom structures its analysis to force both sides
6. The counterintuitive punchline: the bear case is more valuable than the bull case

**Pros:** Strong emotional hook, relatable "I lost money" moment, personal stakes
**Cons:** Might feel too retrospective, risk of sounding like a cautionary tale blog

---

## Outline B: "Steel-Manning Your Way to Better Returns" (Framework-First)

**Hook:** A conversation with a friend who was 100% certain about a stock — and couldn't name a single risk.
1. The problem: most investors are either bulls or bears, never both
2. What steel-manning means and why it's the most underrated investing skill
3. The anatomy of a real bull/bear framework (with specific components)
4. How AI agents eliminate the emotional barrier to arguing against yourself
5. Live walkthrough: CRWD bull vs bear case with real numbers
6. How Bloom forces the bear case into every analysis
7. Why the bear case is where the alpha actually lives

**Pros:** Intellectual framework angle, builds on article 03's CRWD example, more evergreen
**Cons:** Could feel too structured/educational, "steel-manning" might be too niche a term

---

## ✅ Outline C: "Your Portfolio Has a Blind Spot" (Chosen)

**Hook:** Scrolling through my own Bloom watchlist and realizing every single stock had a bullish thesis — and zero had a documented bear case. That's not a portfolio, that's a prayer.
1. The blind spot nobody talks about — why retail investors are structurally biased toward bull cases
2. What confirmation bias actually looks like in practice (specific examples, not textbook definitions)
3. How to prompt an AI to destroy your thesis — and why it's uncomfortable
4. The anatomy of a real bull/bear framework: growth drivers vs risks, base/bull/bear targets, key assumptions
5. Real example: HIMS — walking through both sides with actual numbers
6. How Bloom structures bull vs bear analysis (bottom lines, risk flags, the "what would change my mind" section)
7. The punchline: the bear case is more valuable than the bull case — here's why

**Pros:** Immediate, visceral hook that any investor relates to. Builds naturally from self-awareness to framework to tool. HIMS example connects to article 03. The "blind spot" framing makes it feel urgent, not academic. Ends with a genuinely counterintuitive insight.
**Cons:** None significant — this is the strongest structure.

### Why I chose C:
- The hook is the most visual and immediately relatable — everyone has a watchlist, everyone can picture scrolling through it
- "Blind spot" framing creates urgency without being preachy
- Using HIMS connects to the prior article while showing a new angle (we covered HIMS research in 03, now we go deeper on the bull/bear)
- The progression from "you have a problem" → "here's why" → "here's how to fix it" → "here's what it looks like" → "here's the surprising insight" is the most satisfying narrative arc
