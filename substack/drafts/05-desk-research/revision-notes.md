## Pass 4: Prosody Checker

### Sentence Length Variation
The original had several monotone runs — particularly in the "Where AI Wins" and "Where AI Falls Short" sections where explanatory paragraphs had 4-5 sentences all in the 15-22 word range.

### Fixes Applied
- **Intro:** Added fragment sentences for rhythm. "Three days. One stock." (2+2 words). "Twenty-three more on my watchlist. The math didn't work." (6+5). These break up the longer narrative sentences around them.
- **Desk Research section:** "Screen → gather → read → model → analyze → write." — Keeps the staccato list rhythm. Followed by a medium sentence (time estimates), then the bold standalone punch.
- **$150K Machine:** "Sometimes they do. Mostly? They don't." — Short-short pattern after a longer setup. Question mark adds tonal variety.
- **Filing Review:** List of what's buried in filings uses parallel fragments without verbs ("Risk factors that telegraph... Revenue recognition changes that make... Related party transactions that raise... The MD&A section where..."). Builds rhythm through repetition-with-variation.
- **HIMS section:** Already had good tempo from the minute-by-minute format. Preserved.
- **"Where AI Falls Short":** Each subsection now starts with a bold label then goes: short declarative → specific example → consequence. Consistent rhythm across subsections gives the reader a predictable pattern to ride.
- **Closing:** "Five years ago, three monitors, 1am, one stock memo after three days of work." — All fragments, no verb. Cinematographic. Then "Twenty-three more on the list." Short. Then a full paragraph. Then two short closers.
