# Article #5: Outline Variants
## "Using AI Agents to Automate Your Investment Desk Research"

---

## ✅ OPTION A — "The Junior Analyst Is Dead" (CHOSEN)

**Angle:** Walk through the actual Wall Street desk research workflow step-by-step, show how AI agents replicate each stage, use Bloom as the throughline. Opens with the personal anecdote of Eric doing desk research manually at his old job vs. what he does now.

1. **Hook** — Personal anecdote: spending a weekend building a stock pitch deck / research memo manually, realizing AI could do 80% of the work
2. **What "Desk Research" Actually Means** — Wall Street definition vs. retail investor reality. The junior analyst workflow: screens → filings → models → memo. Why retail investors unknowingly do a crappy version of the same thing.
3. **The $350,000 Junior Analyst Workflow** — Break down what a first-year analyst actually does (specific steps, hours, output). Most of it is mechanical data gathering and formatting, not insight.
4. **How AI Agents Replicate Each Step** — Map the analyst workflow onto AI agent capabilities:
   - Screening → Natural language queries vs. checkbox screeners
   - Data gathering → API calls to financial data sources in seconds
   - Financial modeling → Scenario analysis and valuation sanity checks
   - Qualitative research → Summarizing filings, earnings calls, news
   - Memo writing → Structured bull/bear case generation
5. **A Real Desk Research Session in Bloom** — Full walkthrough of researching a stock from scratch using Bloom, showing the actual workflow and output
6. **The Quality Gap: What AI Does Better** — Speed, breadth, consistency, no ego, doesn't anchor to prior beliefs, covers more ground
7. **The Quality Gap: What AI Still Misses** — Management quality, competitive intuition, regulatory "feel," knowing when numbers lie, relationship-driven intel
8. **Why This Matters If You Don't Have a Research Team** — The democratization angle: individual investors now have access to analyst-quality workflow without the team. Not dumbed-down — the actual workflow, automated.
9. **The Pillar Post Tease** — Brief forward references to deeper dives coming (financial analysis, valuation, screening, insider data — future articles 6-12)
10. **Close** — You're the portfolio manager now. The grunt work is automated. The thinking is still yours.

**Why this outline:** Most comprehensive. Follows the natural "what is it → how did it work → how does AI do it → proof → gaps → implications" arc. The Wall Street comparison gives it authority and specificity. The full walkthrough gives it utility.

---

## OPTION B — "I Hired a Research Team for $20/Month"

**Angle:** Frame the entire article as "what it would cost to replicate Bloom's capabilities with human labor" — salary comparisons, time comparisons, output comparisons. More provocative, more numbers-driven.

1. **Hook** — The job posting: "Junior equity research analyst, $85K-$120K, 80-hour weeks"
2. **What the job posting is really asking for** — Break down the actual tasks
3. **The $20/month version** — Each task mapped to AI agent capability, with cost comparison
4. **Where the $20 version wins** — Speed, scale, availability
5. **Where you still need the $120K human** — Judgment, relationships, intuition
6. **The real punchline** — You don't need the human OR the AI to replace each other. You need the AI to give you (the retail investor) the human's workflow.
7. **Bloom walkthrough** — How this works in practice
8. **What's coming next** — Series tease

**Pros:** Very punchy, great hook, strong numbers angle. **Cons:** Might feel too "marketing" — the cost comparison could veer into salesy territory. Less comprehensive on the "how" side.

---

## OPTION C — "The Research Memo Nobody Wrote"

**Angle:** Open with a specific stock that Eric missed because he didn't have time to research it. Then show how AI desk research would have caught it. Reconstruct the research process retroactively.

1. **Hook** — The stock Eric didn't buy because the research would have taken too long (specific ticker, specific missed gain)
2. **Why he didn't research it** — The time cost of proper desk research for a retail investor
3. **What "proper" desk research looks like** — The Wall Street workflow
4. **Reconstructing the research with AI** — Run the full analysis retroactively, show what the AI would have surfaced
5. **The signals that were there** — Insider buying, margin expansion, catalyst timeline — all findable with AI agents
6. **The broader point** — Every retail investor has a graveyard of stocks they didn't research. AI changes the math.
7. **How to set up your own desk research workflow** — Bloom + process
8. **What AI misses** — Honest quality gap discussion
9. **Series tease**

**Pros:** Very compelling narrative structure — regret is a powerful hook. Specific and concrete. **Cons:** Requires a real example that holds up to scrutiny. Narrower than a true pillar post — it's built around one story rather than the comprehensive workflow.

---

## Decision

**Going with Option A.** It's the most comprehensive, which is right for a pillar post. It maps the Wall Street workflow onto AI capabilities systematically, which gives it authority and utility. The other options are strong hooks but work better as standalone articles, not as the anchor post that the rest of the series links back to.
