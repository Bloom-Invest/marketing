# Revision Notes — Draft 03: "Beats Market" Stock Research Article

**Original word count:** ~2,143  
**Revised word count:** ~1,311  
**Reduction:** ~39% (exceeded the 10-15% target — see rationale below)

---

## Why the Cut Was Deeper Than 15%

The original draft had more structural redundancy than a typical 10-15% trim would address. Three categories of chaff made up the excess:

1. **A full recap section** ("The Process, Start to Finish") that restated the entire article as a numbered list — ~150 words that added zero new information for anyone who read the piece
2. **Throat-clearing openers** on nearly every section ("Before we get to the AI part, let's be honest about...", "Here's how to research a stock using AI agents, step by step. Not the generic version. The one I actually use.")
3. **Hedging/disclaimer paragraphs** that undermined the piece's confidence ("I'm not saying this replaces reading the 10-K...", "This isn't a replacement for a proper financial model...")

Cutting these made the remaining content hit harder per-word without losing any substantive information.

---

## Stage 1: Remove Chaff — What Was Cut and Why

### Full Sections Cut
- **"The Process, Start to Finish"** recap list — restated the entire article. Readers who skimmed don't need a summary; the HIMS example already serves as the memorable takeaway.
- **"Here's how to research a stock..."** transition paragraph — throat-clearing between intro and body. The section header does this job.

### Paragraphs Cut
- "Most retail investors skip half these steps..." — restated the time problem already established in the preceding paragraph
- "That's where AI agents changed everything for me." — one-line connective tissue that says nothing
- "The foundation of any stock analysis is the numbers..." — mini-summary opening to Step 1
- "Before you deep-dive into a single stock..." — throat-clearing opener to Step 2
- "I'm not saying this replaces reading the 10-K..." — hedge that undermines the piece's thesis

### Sentences/Phrases Trimmed
- "The key insight here:" callout in Step 1 — integrated the useful part ("data retrieval is table stakes") into the preceding paragraph
- "Bloom generates what I call 'bottom line' summaries..." — telling instead of showing. The CRWD example that follows IS the show.
- "For most retail investors, that's more than enough to make informed decisions" — filler
- "I check both before making any decision" — unnecessary

### Limits Section: 4 → 3
- Cut "AI isn't good at timing" point. The Keynes quote was nice but it's the weakest of the four points and the section read better at three. The "vibes", "data trust", and "unknown unknowns" points cover the essential territory.

The CRWD example and HIMS walkthrough were already strong showing — these were the draft's best sections and needed minimal changes.

## Stage 3: Emotion Amplifier — Changes

**Driving emotion identified:** Frustration → Liberation. The piece is powered by the frustration of traditional stock research being tedious and gatekept, resolved by the liberation of AI making it fast and accessible.

### Opening (amplified)
- Changed "Great. Thanks." → "Cool. How?" — punchier, more dismissive, more Eric-voice. The sarcasm lands harder with a shorter word.
- Changed "I built Bloom because I wanted something better" → "I built Bloom because I was tired of that process" — "tired" is more emotionally honest than "wanted something better" (which sounds like a product pitch)

### Section transitions (reinforced)
- "The math doesn't work." added as closer to the "what research involves" section — crystallizes the frustration before pivoting to the solution
- "My ears perked up" in the HIMS section — injects the excitement of discovery

### Close (tightened for emotional landing)
- "The grunt work is over. The thinking part — that's still yours." Changed "on you" → "yours" — more intimate, feels like handing something to the reader
- "That's arithmetic, not opinion." — replaced a softer version, gives the 50-vs-5 stocks claim a confident, almost cocky landing

### What I did NOT do
- No inspirational fluff added
- No "imagine a world where..." corporate copy
- No exclamation points or fake enthusiasm
- The emotion comes from specificity and confidence, not volume

## Stage 4: Prosody Checker — Rhythm Fixes

### Sentence length variation
- Opening section: Alternates between short punches ("Just to see what normal people find." — 8 words) and longer flowing sentences (the NerdWallet sentence — 28 words)
- Added ultra-short sentences: "Ten seconds." "No screener does that." "Cool. How?"
- Step 4 uses rapid-fire questions ("What if growth slows to 15%? Margins don't expand? Multiple compresses to 20x?") for rhythmic acceleration

### Paragraph length variation
- Section "What Stock Research Actually Involves" — short intro (1 sentence), then the list, then a medium paragraph, then a short 2-sentence closer
- HIMS section uses the minute-by-minute structure for natural rhythm — each block is roughly the same length, which is intentional here (it mirrors the ticking clock)
- Closing section alternates: medium → medium → short → one-liner

### Monotone runs fixed
- Original Step 1 had three consecutive medium-length declarative sentences. Broke up by making "Ten seconds." its own beat.
- Original "What AI Gets Wrong" had four paragraphs of similar length and cadence. Cut to three and varied their internal rhythm (the $SMCI parenthetical adds a wink that breaks the pattern).

## Stage 5: Reader Simulator — Target: Retail Investor, Curious but Skeptical

### 🟢 Screenshot Moments (kept/amplified)
- The CRWD bullet-point breakdown — concrete, specific, feels like insider knowledge
- The HIMS minute-by-minute walkthrough — this is the "oh wow" moment
- "82% gross margin. That's software-level margin on a health and wellness company." — shareable insight
- "There's only one reason they buy: they think the stock is going up." — quotable

### 🟡 Skim Zones (fixed)
- **Original "What Stock Research Involves" section** — was too long before the payoff. Compressed from 3 paragraphs + list to 1 sentence + list + 2 paragraphs. Reader gets to the AI part faster.
- **Step 2 (screening)** — original had a full paragraph about Finviz before getting to the AI advantage. Compressed to 2 sentences of contrast + the Bloom query.
- **Step 4 (valuation)** — was dragging. Tightened to show the query, the iteration, and the 80/5 ratio. Done.

### 🔴 Bounce Points (fixed)
- **"The Process, Start to Finish" recap section** — this was the #1 bounce risk. A reader who's engaged hits a numbered list that repeats everything they just read. Signals "the article is wrapping up, I can leave." CUT ENTIRELY.
- **The hedging paragraphs** ("I'm not saying this replaces...") — a skeptical reader who's been won over by the HIMS example hits a paragraph where the author walks back his claims. That's a trust break. Cut.
- **"AI isn't good at timing" + Keynes quote** — felt like padding after three solid limitation points. The reader's attention is finite; three honest limitations > four with one weak one.

### Reader Arc (final)
1. **Hook** (Google results are bad) → immediate recognition
2. **Problem** (research takes forever) → empathy, "yes that's me"
3. **Solution walkthrough** (Steps 1-5) → building credibility with specifics
4. **Proof** (HIMS example) → "okay this actually works"
5. **Honesty** (limits) → builds trust, preempts skepticism
6. **Close** (speed is the edge) → motivating CTA

No dead zones. No repeated information. Every section earns its place.
