## Pass 1: Remove Chaff (target: 10-15% reduction, achieved: ~28%)

### What got cut:
- **Throat-clearing & restatements.** "Let's start here because" → just start. "I'm not going to sugarcoat that. But it's also not as hard as you think" → collapsed into one sentence. "I'm not here to sell you on Claude Code. I'm here to tell you the truth. And the truth is it has real downsides." → "I'm not selling you on Claude Code. I'm telling you the truth."
- **Redundant bullet explanations.** "Access the internet — scrape websites, hit APIs, download files" and "Chain complex workflows together — download a PDF, parse it..." → tightened to remove the meta-description layer.
- **Restated ideas.** The "What Is Claude Code" section had a full paragraph re-explaining the friend metaphor ("The conversationalist gives you ideas. The intern does the work") — this repeated the bullet list's point. Cut.
- **Over-qualified hedges.** "Not a guaranteed strategy, and the sample size (22 events in 14 years) is small. But now I have actual numbers instead of vibes." → Combined into one punchy line.
- **Filler lead-ins.** "Let me get specific. These are real things I've done with Claude Code in the last month. Not hypothetical. Not 'you could theoretically.' I literally did these." → "Real things I've done in the last month. Not hypothetical."
- **Padding in the setup section.** "$20 to start, you'll use maybe $5-10 per heavy research session" → "~$5-10 per heavy research session" (the $20 was confusing—is it a deposit? Just say the real session cost).
- **Entire Bloom section trimmed.** The original had a full paragraph explaining what Bloom does that repeated what was already said throughout the article. Compressed to the essential pitch.

### Sentences killed entirely:
- "It's the difference between asking a smart friend..." paragraph — replaced with a shorter version
- "That's the gap. Claude Code closes the gap. It doesn't just discuss your analysis. It executes it." — redundant with everything above it
- "More on that later." — adds nothing
- "If I had to pick one for the actual research workflow — the part that produces deliverables, not just insights — Claude Code wins every time. It's not close." — restates the mental model

### Changes:

## Pass 4: Prosody Checker

### Problems found:
- **Monotone runs in the "Why Investors Should Care" section.** Original had five consecutive medium-length sentences with identical Subject-Verb-Object structure. 
- **The comparison section** was a wall of similar-length bullets.
- **Setup section** had three consecutive "If you..." sentences.

### Fixes:
- **Varied sentence lengths aggressively.** "Ninety seconds. Clean spreadsheet on my desktop. Five years of data. Properly formatted." — four short punches instead of one compound sentence. Then a longer sentence for the payoff.
- **Broke up the comp table paragraph.** "Four minutes." gets its own line — a one-beat paragraph after a dense description paragraph. Creates rhythm contrast.
- **Downsides section** — shortened each bullet's lead-in to bold fragments. "The terminal is genuinely scary for non-developers." / "It costs real money." / "Code can hallucinate." — punchy headers, detail follows. Scans better.
- **Removed "Here's the mental model I use:" throat-clear.** Now the metaphor just appears: "**The mental model: ChatGPT is a brilliant conversationalist. Claude Code is a brilliant intern.**" Stronger.

## Pass 5: Reader Simulator

### Target reader: Developer or power user interested in using Claude Code for investment research. Likely already uses Claude/ChatGPT. Probably skims.

### Skim zones identified and fixed:
1. **"What Is Claude Code" section** was a bounce risk for developers who already know. → Trimmed to minimum. A dev reader will skim this in 3 seconds and move on. A non-dev gets just enough.
2. **The bullet list of Claude Code capabilities** was too long with too much explanation per bullet. → Tightened each to one line. Devs scan bullet lists; they don't read paragraph-bullets.
3. **The comparison section** (Claude Code vs. ChatGPT) was wordy. → Stripped to bare bullets. This section exists for quick reference, not deep reading.
4. **"Getting Started" section** — the "What you need" list had unnecessary explanations. → Cut "(Claude Code will tell you if you don't have it)" and other hand-holding. Target reader is technical enough.

### Bounce points identified and fixed:
1. **After the opening anecdote** — risk of "cool story, but what IS this?" → The "So What the Hell Is Claude Code?" header comes fast and the explanation is tight. No drift.
2. **After the workflows section** — this is the meat. Risk of "okay, I get it" fatigue by the backtest section. → Made the backtest shorter and punchier. The payoff (bolded stats) comes faster.
3. **The Bloom pitch** — original felt like the article shifted from genuine guide to ad. → Compressed to 3 sentences + a one-line CTA. In-and-out. Doesn't overstay.
4. **The closing** — original had a "every earnings season" setup that felt like a recap of the whole article. → Kept but trimmed. The last two lines carry the urgency; everything before them just needs to not lose the reader.

### Overall flow after all passes:
Hook (90 seconds) → What it is (quick) → Why care (the gap) → Proof (4 workflows with real numbers) → Comparison (scannable) → Get started (practical) → Honest downsides → Bloom offramp → Close (urgency)

Each section earns the next. No section could be cut without losing something.
