# Article #11: "Using AI Agents to Identify Key Drivers" — Outline Variants

## Variant A: "The Netflix Earnings Night" (Opening with a real-time market reaction)

**Hook:** Watching Netflix after-hours — stock rips 14% not because of EPS beat but because subscriber adds crushed expectations. That's when it clicked: EPS didn't move this stock. One number did.

1. **Hook** — Netflix earnings night, price moves on subs not EPS
2. **What "key drivers" actually means** — the 2-3 metrics that explain 80%+ of a stock's price movement. Not EPS, not revenue — the business-specific lever.
3. **Why most investors focus on the wrong thing** — EPS is a lagging indicator, Wall Street headline obsession, the "earnings beat" trap
4. **How to find key drivers the old way** — reading transcripts, tracking what management emphasizes, noting what analysts ask about repeatedly. Time-consuming and manual.
5. **How AI agents change the game** — parsing transcripts at scale, tracking emphasis across quarters, extracting what management repeats vs. what they dodge
6. **Building a "driver scorecard"** — a simple framework: identify 2-3 drivers per stock, track them quarter over quarter, set alerts for inflections
7. **Case studies** — Amazon (AWS growth rate), Chipotle (same-store sales), CrowdStrike (net new ARR), Netflix (subscriber adds), Meta (ad impressions + price per ad)
8. **How Bloom does this** — natural plug for the product
9. **Close** — the real alpha is knowing what to watch, not watching everything

## ✅ Variant B: "The Chipotle Epiphany" (Opening with a specific trade/realization) ← CHOSEN

**Hook:** Staring at Chipotle's earnings — they "missed" on EPS but same-store sales were up 7.9%. Stock popped. That's the moment I stopped caring about earnings per share.

1. **Hook** — Chipotle earnings: EPS miss, stock goes up. WTF moment that teaches the lesson.
2. **The dirty secret of stock prices** — most stocks are driven by 1-2 metrics, and EPS usually isn't one of them. The market is a pricing machine for *specific* business drivers.
3. **Why EPS is a lagging indicator** — it's an output, not an input. By the time it shows up in EPS, the market already priced it. What moves stocks is the *leading* metrics.
4. **What key drivers look like for real companies** — Amazon: AWS growth rate. Netflix: paid net adds. Chipotle: same-store sales + new store openings. CrowdStrike: net new ARR. Meta: average revenue per user. Specific, concrete examples.
5. **How to find them (the manual way sucks)** — read 3-4 quarters of earnings transcripts, note what management leads with, what analysts ask about first, what the stock reacts to. This takes hours per stock.
6. **How AI agents flip this** — feed an agent 8 quarters of transcripts and ask "what does management emphasize most?" Track keyword frequency, sentiment around specific metrics, compare management emphasis vs. analyst focus.
7. **The driver scorecard** — practical framework anyone can build. 2-3 drivers per stock, quarterly tracking, delta from consensus, and a "surprise direction" column.
8. **One driver, 80% of the move** — deep examples where tracking one metric would have predicted the stock move. Meta's "year of efficiency" (cost cuts → margins), Netflix's password sharing crackdown (subscriber inflection).
9. **How Bloom fits in** — this is what AI-powered research actually looks like. Not generic summaries — tracking the specific metrics that matter for each stock.
10. **Close** — Stop watching everything. Start watching the right thing.

## Decision Notes

**Chose Variant B** because:
- The Chipotle "EPS miss but stock pops" hook is a concrete, specific moment that most investors have experienced (or can relate to)
- It immediately teaches the core lesson in the opening anecdote
- The "WTF" reaction is emotionally engaging and matches the blog's tone
- Variant A's Netflix hook is good but more observational; Variant B has more tension
- Variant C's information overload hook is relatable but less punchy — it starts with a problem rather than a surprising moment
